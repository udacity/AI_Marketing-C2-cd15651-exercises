# Demo Output — Delegate the Research, Then Audit What Comes Back

*Actual Claude output — a real web-sourced mini research run, then a source-quality audit of two claims. One run; live sources change over time.*

## The delegation brief handed to the agent

> Q: competitive landscape + market trends for smart/hydration-tracking bottles, for Vessl's launch. Scope: premium reusable + smart entrants — positioning, pricing, app/subscription, features, gaps; plus category trend. Output: short brief, a source (link + date) per claim. Guardrails: credible + recent sources; flag the unverifiable; stop when scope covered.

## Returned brief (excerpt)

> - Premium reusable bottles cluster around **$29–$55** (Hydro Flask 18oz ~$29 → 40oz ~$55; Owala FreeSip 32oz ~$35).
> - Smart/tracking entrant **HidrateSpark PRO** runs **~$70–$85** at list — a clear price premium for tracking.
> - The smart water bottle market is **growing ~12% a year**.

## Audit of two claims (the point of the demo)

**Claim that holds up — pricing.**
> Checked directly: Hydro Flask and Owala list prices match multiple current retailer pages; HidrateSpark list prices match its own store and Apple/Amazon. Real, credible, current → **Trust.**

**Claim that's weak — "~12% CAGR."**
> Opened the sources: reputable-looking firms disagree wildly — Fortune Business Insights ~**7.49%** ($585M→$1.04B), MarketResearchFuture ~**10.7%** ($3.2B→$8.9B), market.us ~**12.3%** ($40.2B→$128.2B), ResearchNester ~**19.5%** ($149M→$886M). Base-year sizes differ by **~100×**. A single "~12%" hides a range of 6.75%–19.5% and incompatible market definitions → **Verify-first / don't cite as fact.**

**Marked-up verdict:** trust the pricing; do not put the growth stat in the CMO deck without picking one credible, clearly-scoped source. A confident brief is a starting point, not a source of truth.
