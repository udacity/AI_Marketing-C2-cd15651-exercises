# Demo Output — Delegate the Research, Then Audit What Comes Back

*The clean deliverable the demo produces. The [`walkthrough.md`](walkthrough.md) narrates how; this is just the artifact. A real run pulls live sources; the audited claims below illustrate the method. Illustrative.*

## The delegation brief handed to the agent

> **Question:** competitive landscape + market trends for the smart/hydration-tracking bottle category, for Vessl's launch.
> **Scope:** 4–6 brands; for each: positioning, target, rough pricing, app/subscription, standout features, gaps. Plus 3–4 category trends.
> **Output:** short brief; a source (link + date) for every meaningful claim.
> **Guardrails:** credible + recent sources; flag the unverifiable; stop when scope is covered.

## Audit of two returned claims (the point of the demo)

**Claim that holds up:**
> "Premium reusable bottles retail ~$35–50." → checked retailer listings; prices match; recent. **Trust.**

**Claim that's weak:**
> "The smart-bottle market is growing ~15% a year." → cited an unnamed "industry report," no link, figure unfindable. **Verify-first** (don't put in the CMO deck until confirmed).

## Marked-up verdict

Per claim: **trust** the well-sourced pricing; **verify-first** the growth stat; a polished, confident brief is a *starting point*, not a source of truth. You own what you forward.
