## The delegation brief handed to the agent

> **Q:** which sustainability claims premium reusable-bottle brands make about materials and packaging, and how much of each claim is independently backed — to inform Vessl's launch sustainability messaging.
> **Scope:** 3–4 category brands. Per brand: the material claim, the packaging claim, and what (if anything) certifies it. Plus the certification schemes that recur in this category and what each actually certifies, plus consumer research on whether these claims move purchase.
> **Output:** short brief; a source (link + the source's own date if shown + date retrieved) for every claim; name **who is asserting** each claim.
> **Guardrails:** primary and current sources preferred; keep the brand's own word separate from an independently certified fact; flag anything unverifiable; skip positioning and pricing; stop when the scope is covered.

## Audit of four claims (the point of the demo)

The verdict follows the rule in the checklist: **Trust** = all four checks pass (real, credible for the claim — primary or clearly attributed, current as of the date recorded, supported when you open it) — safe to cite as a fact; **Verify-first** = mixed checks — usable as directional, confirm against a primary source before citing it as a number; **Drop** = two or more checks fail, or no source can be identified.

| Claim | Cited source | Accessed | Credible? | Verified against source? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1. [Brand A]'s recycled-steel claim + [Certification Scheme] status | [Brand A]'s own sustainability page + the scheme's public directory | [YYYY-MM-DD] | Yes — primary for the claim, the certifier for the certificate | Yes — page says it, directory lists the brand | **Trust** |
| 2. "The category is moving to plastic-free packaging" | named trade outlet, article dated ~2 years earlier | [YYYY-MM-DD] | Yes — real trade reporting | Yes, as written — but the reporting is two years stale | **Verify-first** |
| 3. "[N]% will pay more for sustainable packaging" | [Research Firm]'s own survey release | [YYYY-MM-DD] | Yes — the firm publishing its own survey | Partly — the survey asked about "sustainable products" globally, across categories | **Verify-first** |
| 4. "[N]% of returned smart bottles are landfilled" | "[Year] [Industry] Packaging White Paper" | [YYYY-MM-DD] — 404, nothing to open | No — no publisher, no author, no document | Couldn't check — the source doesn't exist | **Drop** |

**1 — trust it for exactly what it proves.** Both sources open: the brand's own page makes the recycled-content claim, and the scheme's public directory shows the brand with a live certificate. Real, credible (primary for a claim about itself; the certifier is independent for the certificate), current as of the date recorded, and both say what the brief says → **Trust.** The discipline is in the wording: this supports *"[Brand A] claims recycled content and holds [Certification Scheme]"*, not *"[Brand A]'s recycled content is independently audited"* — only whatever sits inside that scheme's scope is.

**2 — right, but old.** The article says precisely what the brief says. Three checks pass; **current** fails — it's roughly two years old, and announced packaging switches slip, get scaled back, or get re-announced. One check down → **Verify-first: confirm the switch actually shipped before saying the category has moved.**

**3 — real source, drifted claim.** The release is real, current, and published by the firm that ran the survey. **Supported** is where it breaks: the question was about "sustainable products" across categories worldwide, and the brief has re-labelled it as packaging in this category. That's a different number about a different thing → **Verify-first: use it as a direction, don't put the figure on a slide.**

**4 — the most citable-looking line, and the one that has to go.** It reads like the strongest claim in the brief: a titled, year-stamped white paper and a precise percentage. The link 404s, and the title returns nothing anywhere under any publisher — no author, no issuing body, no document. **Real** fails, and **supported** can't even be attempted → **Drop.** Note what that does and doesn't mean: e-waste from battery-powered products is a reasonable thing to worry about, and this audit says nothing either way about it. It says there is no source here, so you cannot make the claim yet. Re-source it to a named, checkable publisher, or cut it — don't soften the wording and keep it.

**Marked-up verdict:** ship claim 1, worded as what [Brand A] claims and holds. Re-check claim 2 before saying the category has shifted. Keep claim 3 as a direction only, out of the slide numbers. Strike claim 4 outright — there's nothing behind it to fix. A confident brief is a starting point, not a source of truth.
