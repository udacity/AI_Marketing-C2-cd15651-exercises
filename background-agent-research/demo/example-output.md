# Demo Output — Delegate the Research, Then Audit What Comes Back

*Actual Claude output — a real web-sourced mini research run, then a source-quality audit of three claims. One run; live sources change over time.*

## The delegation brief handed to the agent

> Q: competitive landscape + market trends for the water bottle market, for Vessl's launch. Scope: premium reusable brands + smart/tracking entrants — positioning, pricing, app/subscription, features, gaps; plus category trend. Output: short brief, a source (link + date retrieved) per claim. Guardrails: credible + recent sources; flag the unverifiable; stop when scope covered.

## Returned brief (excerpt)

> - Premium reusable bottles cluster around **$29–$55** (Hydro Flask 18oz ~$29 → 40oz ~$55; Owala FreeSip 32oz ~$35). *(Hydro Flask + Owala own product pages — accessed 2026-09-01)*
> - Smart/tracking entrant **HidrateSpark PRO** runs **~$70–$85** at list — a clear price premium for tracking. *(HidrateSpark store, Apple, Amazon — accessed 2026-09-01)*
> - The smart water bottle market is **growing ~12% a year**. *(four market-research firms — accessed 2026-09-02)*
> - Smart/tracking bottles are **about 15% of premium bottle sales**, and **HidrateSpark holds ~60%** of that segment. *("Top 12 Smart Water Bottles of 2026" roundup — accessed 2026-09-03)*

## Audit of three claims (the point of the demo)

The verdict follows the rule in the checklist: **Trust** = all four checks pass (real, credible for the claim — primary or clearly attributed, current as of the date recorded, supported when you open it) — safe to cite as a fact; **Verify-first** = mixed checks — usable as directional, confirm against a primary source before citing it as a number; **Drop** = two or more checks fail, or no source can be identified.

**Claim that holds up — pricing.**
> Checked directly on 2026-09-01: Hydro Flask and Owala list prices match the manufacturers' own product pages; HidrateSpark list prices match its own store and Apple/Amazon. Real, primary and credible, current as of that date, and the sources say exactly what the brief claims — all four checks pass → **Trust.**

**Claim that's weak — "~12% CAGR."**
> Opened the sources on 2026-09-02: reputable-looking firms disagree wildly — Fortune Business Insights ~**7.49%** ($585M→$1.04B), MarketResearchFuture ~**10.7%** ($3.2B→$8.9B), market.us ~**12.3%** ($40.2B→$128.2B), ResearchNester ~**19.5%** ($149M→$886M). Base-year sizes differ by **~100×**. A single "~12%" hides a range of 6.75%–19.5% and incompatible market definitions. The checks come back mixed — the sources are real and dated, but the scopes don't match and no one of them supports the stated number → **Verify-first: the market is clearly growing, but pick one clearly-scoped source before quoting a rate.**

**Claim that has to go — "15% of premium sales, 60% share."**
> It read like the most citable line in the brief: a 2026-stamped "Top 12 Smart Water Bottles of 2026" roundup, clean comparison tables, both share figures stated to the point. Opening it on 2026-09-03 showed what the formatting was hiding — no author byline, no masthead, no "about" page, just an "editorial team" credit and a contact form, and the same paragraphs running verbatim on three near-identical bottle-review domains. The share numbers carry no attribution at all: "industry data suggests." There is no identifiable original publisher to check, and an aggregator that attributes nothing is not credible for a market-share figure — **real** fails and **credible** fails, two checks down → **Drop: cut the share figures, or re-source them to a named firm that publishes the segment split.**

**Marked-up verdict:** trust the pricing; do not put the growth stat in the CMO deck without first verifying it against one credible, clearly-scoped source; strike the share figures entirely — there is no source behind them to fix. A confident brief is a starting point, not a source of truth.
