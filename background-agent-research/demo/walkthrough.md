# Worked Demo — Delegate the Research, Then Audit What Comes Back

*The real content: the finished walkthrough the demo produces.*

## Delegating is goals-not-steps with autonomy

Start from the CMO's request in [`research-task-brief.md`](../exercise/starter/research-task-brief.md) ("competitive landscape + market trends for smart hydration bottles") and write it into a clear agent brief:

- **The question** — exactly what to answer.
- **The scope** — which competitors, and what dimensions (positioning, pricing, features, market trends).
- **The output format** — a short brief with a source for every claim.
- **The guardrails** — prefer credible and recent sources, flag anything it can't verify, stop when the brief is covered.

## Let it run, then read once for usefulness

Set the background agent running and let it work autonomously. When it returns, read the brief once to see whether it's useful. An autonomous agent always returns a confident, complete-looking brief — but completeness is not credibility.

## The audit is the real work

Pick two or three key claims and open the cited sources. For each, check three things: is the source real and credible, is it current, and does it actually support the claim?

- **One that holds up:** a pricing or market figure backed by a reputable, current source that says exactly that. Trust it.
- **One that's weak** *(illustrative — the actual weak claim depends on what the agent returns)*: a confident statistic with a vague "studies show," a dead citation, or a real source used to back a claim it never makes. Verify-first or drop.

## Mark up the brief

Per claim: **trust**, **verify-first**, or **drop**. You own the brief you pass on, not the agent.

## Key takeaway

A background agent can do hours of research in minutes, but a polished brief is a starting point, not a source of truth. Delegating well and auditing hard is what makes the output usable.
