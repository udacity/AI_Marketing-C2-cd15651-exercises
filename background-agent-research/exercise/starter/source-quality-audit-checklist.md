# Source-Quality Audit Checklist

*Provided template for the "Delegate, Then Audit for Source Quality" exercise. Use it to audit the brief your agent returns before you trust or forward it.*

An agent's research brief always looks confident and complete. Completeness is not credibility. Your job is to check that the claims are actually backed by real, credible, current sources that say what the brief claims.

---

## For each key claim, check four things

1. **Real?** Does the cited source actually exist, at the link or reference given? Watch for invented or dead citations.
2. **Credible?** Is the source trustworthy for this claim (an industry report, a reputable outlet, the company itself), or is it a low-quality blog, a forum post, or an SEO content farm?
3. **Current?** Is it recent enough to still be true? Record the date you opened the source (and the source's own date, if it shows one), then judge recency from those. Pricing, market size, and competitor claims go stale fast — a figure you last checked weeks ago is not a figure you can cite today.
4. **Supported?** Open it and confirm the source actually says what the brief claims. Watch for numbers that drift, quotes taken out of context, or a real source used to back a claim it never makes.

---

## Red flags to watch for

- A confident statistic with no source, or a vague "studies show."
- A citation you cannot find, or a link that goes nowhere.
- A precise number attributed to a source that does not contain it.
- Outdated figures presented as current.
- The same weak source propping up several claims.

---

## From the four checks to a verdict

Every claim gets exactly one of three verdicts, and the four checks above decide which:

- **Trust** — all four checks pass: the source is real, credible for this claim (primary or clearly attributed), current as of the date you recorded, and it says what the brief claims when you open it. Safe to cite as a fact.
- **Verify-first** — the checks come back mixed. Usable as a directional read; confirm it against a primary source before you cite it as a number.
- **Drop** — two or more checks fail, or you cannot identify a usable source at all. Don't cite it: replace the source or cut the claim.

Use these three labels exactly. "Looks fine overall" is not a verdict.
