# Worked Demo — Score Three Creative Directions, Rank the Winner

*The real content: the finished walkthrough the demo produces.*

## The three directions

For Barkwell's orthopedic senior-dog bed line:

- **A — "Comfort for their golden years"** — emotional/aging angle.
- **B — "Vet-recommended orthopedic support"** — clinical/credibility angle.
- **C — "The bed they'll actually sleep in"** — practical/outcome angle.

Tested across three senior-dog-owner personas from [`persona-panel-senior-dog.md`](persona-panel-senior-dog.md).

## Score on fixed dimensions

Scoring on fixed dimensions turns three gut reactions into a comparable ranking. Run each direction past each persona and capture 1–5 on:

- **Resonance** — does it feel emotionally true to this person?
- **Believability** — does it make them trust the brand more?
- **Relevance** — does it speak to their actual reason to buy?
- **Distinctiveness** — does it feel different from what they've seen?

## Aggregate

Build a simple table — totals by concept and by persona. The aggregation is where the recommendation lives: the same scores can support "clear winner" or "too close to call," and honesty about which is the actual skill.

## Name the winner *and* the confidence

State the front-runner, then the confidence level and why: how wide is the spread between concepts, and how much did the personas agree? A tight spread with disagreement is low confidence, however clean the winner looks.

## State the limits

Out loud: a 3-persona synthetic panel is directional, not statistically meaningful; synthetic reasoning over-weights logic versus gut reaction; and these are brief creative descriptions, not finished ads. This test's job is to kill the weak concepts and surface a front-runner to validate for real — not to crown a winner.

## Key takeaway

A synthetic A/B test produces a ranked recommendation you can defend, but only if you pair the ranking with a stated confidence level and honest limits.
