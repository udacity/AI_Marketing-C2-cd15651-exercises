# Solution — Run the Test, Deliver the Recommendation

*Worked solution — one strong example. The scores below are illustrative of a plausible run (synthetic panels vary between runs); a student's numbers will differ. What matters: fresh-context scoring, honest aggregation, a stated confidence basis, panel-split awareness, and a real next step.*

**Directions:** A = design-magazine aesthetic · B = "your dog deserves nice things too" (indulgence) · C = "a dog bed you won't hide when guests come over" (honesty).
**Panel:** Maya (design apartment) · Rick (practical multi-dog) · Priya (new puppy).

## Scoring table (illustrative; 1–5 per dimension, totals /20)

| Persona | A total | B total | C total | Top pick |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Maya | 18 | 12 | 16 | A |
| Rick | 8 | 7 | 13 | C |
| Priya | 13 | 15 | 14 | B |
| **Avg** | **13.0** | **11.3** | **14.3** | — |

## Aggregation

- **Winner: Direction C ("won't hide when guests come over")** — avg 14.3. Strongest on *relevance* (it names a real, universal moment); most vulnerable on *distinctiveness*.
- **Runner-up: A** — avg 13.0, margin 1.3. Wins big with Maya, loses badly with Rick.
- **Third: B** — avg 11.3.
- **Panel split:** A polarizes hard (Maya 18 / Rick 8). C is the most *consistent* across very different owners.

## Confidence

**Medium-low.** Basis: the top two (C 14.3, A 13.0) are within ~1.3 points — too close to call a decisive winner — and A's result depends entirely on which audience you weight. C wins on *consistency*, not margin.

## Stated limits

- A 3-persona synthetic panel is directional, not statistically meaningful.
- Synthetic responses over-weight reasoning vs. gut reaction.
- These are brief creative *descriptions*, not finished ads.

## Next step (validation)

Advance **C** to a real-world test — it's the safe, broadly-relevant front-runner. But run **A** as a *targeted* play to the design-conscious segment specifically (it's a winner for Maya-type owners, a loser for everyone else). Test with real customers: a small paid-social A/B of C vs. A creative against a design-minded urban audience, measuring click-through and add-to-cart, before committing budget.

## Common mistakes

- Scoring all three directions in one conversation (anchoring) — run each persona fresh.
- Declaring a "clear winner" when the top two are within noise.
- Reporting the winner without confidence + limits.
- Missing that A is a *polarizing targeted play*, not a safe default.
