# Vessl — Brand Voice Guide

*Provided asset for this module. Use it as the brand's source of truth for tone, language, and look.*

---

## Brand snapshot

**What Vessl is:** a smart water bottle that tracks your hydration in real time. Sensors in the bottle measure how much you drink, the companion app shows where you stand against your daily goal, and gentle reminders nudge you when you fall behind.

**What we believe:** staying hydrated shouldn't take willpower or math. Your body does enough already. Vessl handles the tracking and the nudging so you can just feel good and get on with your day.

**One-line positioning:** Hydration, handled.

---

## Who we talk to

Active, health-aware people, roughly 25 to 45, who already pay some attention to how they feel: they work out, they wear a watch that counts their steps or tracks their sleep, and they like the idea of one less thing to think about. They are motivated, busy, and a little skeptical of wellness hype. They want help, not a lecture.

---

## Personality

Vessl sounds like a supportive friend who happens to be organized, not a coach, a doctor, or a guru.

- **Encouraging**, never nagging.
- **Clear**, never clinical.
- **Upbeat**, with a light sense of humor, never goofy.
- **Confident**, never boastful.

---

## Voice and tone

- Talk to the reader directly, as "you."
- Keep sentences short and plain. If a line sounds like a spec sheet, rewrite it.
- Be warm and motivating. Celebrate small wins ("nice, you're ahead of schedule") instead of flagging failures.
- Lead with how the person feels, then mention the tech. The feeling is the product; the sensors are how we deliver it.
- A little wit is welcome. A pun about water is fine in moderation.

**Tone shifts by moment:**
- **App nudges:** friendly and brief, like a tap on the shoulder.
- **Marketing and social:** aspirational but grounded, focused on the payoff of feeling good.
- **Support and onboarding:** patient and reassuring, plain instructions, zero jargon.

---

## Language

**Words we like:** hydration, on track, your day, a little nudge, feel your best, keep it up, effortless, simple.

**Words and moves we avoid:**
- Fear or guilt: "dangerously dehydrated," "you failed your goal," "you're behind." Motivate, don't shame.
- Clinical or spec-sheet language: "biometric fluid-intake analytics." Say what it does in human terms.
- Wellness mysticism: "align your body's natural rhythms," "unlock your inner vitality."
- Hype: "revolutionary," "game-changing," "the future of water." Let the product feel calm and capable.

---

## On-brand vs. off-brand (quick examples)

| Moment | Off-brand | On-brand |
|---|---|---|
| App nudge | "Warning: hydration critically low." | "A little behind today. Time for a few sips?" |
| Social post | "Revolutionary biometric hydration analytics, now on your desk." | "Your water bottle now keeps score, so you don't have to. Nice." |
| Feature line | "Real-time fluid-intake tracking via embedded sensors." | "It knows how much you've had, and gently reminds you when it's time for more." |

---

## Look and feel (for creative)

- **Overall mood:** clean, bright, and fresh. Lots of open space and natural daylight. Everything should feel hydrated and calm, never cluttered or clinical.
- **Color direction:** cool aquas and soft blues as the core, a clean white base, and one warm accent (a soft coral or amber) for energy and calls to action.
- **Imagery:** real people mid-day and mid-motion, a walk, a desk, a gym bag, with the bottle naturally in frame. Candid over posed. Daylight over studio. Small hydration cues welcome (condensation, a bead of water, a full glass).
- **Type:** a modern, friendly sans-serif. Clean and readable, never techy or futuristic.
- **The bottle is the hero.** When in doubt, show the product in a real moment of someone's day.
