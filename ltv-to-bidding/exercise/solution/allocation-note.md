# Sample Write-Up — What Drives LTV, and One Spend Change

*A sample of the deliverable: a short recommendation answering the four questions and ending with one specific spend change. LTV here is the simple estimate (average observed tenure × average monthly fee) from `steep_cohorts_exercise.csv`. A learner's wording will differ; what matters is the true driver, the confound, and one clear spend change.*

---

**Segments.** Annual plan subscribers are our top segment in terms of highest LTV ($102.74 on average). Monthly plan subscribers are our lowest ($74.34). Annual customers are worth about 38% more.

**Driver.** The biggest driver of value is plan type: the split is by plan commitment, not necessarily high-value types of people.

**Confound.** Income band looked like a driver, but it turns out higher-income customers just had noticeably higher rates of purchasing the annual plan. Within a single plan type, lifetime value remains flat across all income levels. Channel and geography showed nothing discernible.

**Action.** Target and incentivize sign-ups for the annual plan directly: play with the price card on the website and advertise the annual plan in our acquisition channels more heavily. Don't over-index on acquisition channel or geography when deciding how to split spend, and don't shift budget to referral just because it has the lowest acquisition cost. Optimizing for cheap conversions buys the customers who are cheap to buy, not necessarily the most valuable ones.
