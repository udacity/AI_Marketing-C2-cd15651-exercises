# Allocation Note — Bid Up / Bid Down (the human call)

*The marketer's own decision, alongside the automated value signal. Grounded in the LTV analysis, not the demographic story.*

## The call

**Optimize for acquisition type. It is the only variable that moves value.**

- **Bid UP on full-price acquisition — every channel, every geo.** Full-price LTV is ~$316 at ~$22.22 CAC (~14.2×). Discount is ~$52 at ~$19.43 (~2.7×). We pay effectively the same for both and get **6.1× more** from one of them. All three full-price segments clear the high bar (referral 28.3×, search 14.3×, paid_social 11.1×).
- **Bid DOWN on discount acquisition**, hardest on **discount|search (2.3×)** and **discount|paid_social (1.8×)** — both under our 3× bar, i.e. barely paying for themselves once margin is applied. discount|referral (4.6×) is mid: hold and squeeze, don't kill.
- **Do NOT cut IN.** IN's raw LTV (~$69) is an offer-mix artifact: 81% of its customers arrive on discount, vs. 25% in CA. An IN *full-price* customer is worth ~$325 — slightly **more** than a Canadian one (~$311). Cutting the geo throws away good customers to avoid a bad offer. **Fix IN's acquisition mix instead.**
- **Do NOT shift budget to referral because its CPA is lowest.** Referral is our cheapest traffic (~$11.12) *and* our least valuable (~$81 blended) — because the referral offer **is** a discount, so 69% of its volume arrives discounted. Search costs ~2× per customer and returns ~90% more blended LTV. Referral's full-price customers are worth the same as anyone's. **Fix the referral offer, not the referral budget.** Same error as "cut IN," pointed the other way.

## Why this is the human's job, not the platform's

The platform will optimize hard against whatever value signal we upload — that part is automatic. What it cannot do is **change the referral offer** or **rebalance IN's acquisition mix**. It can only chase the customers our current offers produce. Deciding to stop manufacturing discount customers is a marketing decision, and it's ours.

## Limits

- **Young cohorts carry the most forecast risk** — treat the newest cohort's LTV as a range, not a number.
- LTV is **pooled within acquisition type**: segment-level churn is underpowered below ~500 customers, so the segments differ on CAC, not on modelled lifetime. Re-split once volume supports it.
- Watch whether pulling back on discount shrinks volume faster than it lifts blended LTV. Test it; don't assume it.
