# Demo Output — Score One Tool Against the Framework

*Claude's output from the demo run: a simulated Draftwell pilot scored against the six dimensions, then a check of how a lean team's context changes what matters. One run; outputs vary.*

**Tool scored:** **Draftwell**, a fictional AI copywriting tool for email. **The team:** a lean startup with two people covering lifecycle and email. Scale 1 to 5, 5 being best.

**Simulated pilot (five-week free trial):** draft-to-send time dropped from three hours to 45 minutes. A/B tests showed a small, not really significant open-rate lift and a meaningful click-rate lift. A couple of factual errors slipped out. Onboarding took under a day.

| Dimension | AI score | Rationale |
|---|---|---|
| Capability fit | 4 | Good on high-volume, formulaic emails; weak on nuance or humor-driven copy. |
| Fit with your stack | 4 | Native integration with the email platform, which is helpful for a lean team, but it doesn't extend further. |
| Cost vs. value | 4 | Saved a lot of time. No cost was specified, so the value back to the team is time saved rather than a clear business impact. |
| Data & privacy | 3 | A flag, depending on your organization; larger enterprises have much stricter rules here. |
| Learning curve | 5 | Under one day of onboarding and intuitive brief templates. |
| Brand-voice control & brand safety | 3 | Didn't handle tone all that well, and produced a few factual hallucinations. |

**Challenging a score:** the AI's score is not automatically your score. Cost vs. value is the one to push back on: the pilot saved time, but there is no business impact to point to, and the price is unknown. **Scored 4 by the AI; recalibrated to 3.**

**Context check (lean team, little product or engineering support, little copywriting support):** fit with your stack matters more, because there are few internal resources to handle an integration. Cost vs. value shifts, because internal capability to build something like this isn't strong. Learning curve matters more, because a lean team can't afford to spend a lot of time learning a new tool. Data and privacy and brand-voice control become go/no-go questions.

**The call stays with you:** Claude lays out the options rather than making the decision. Total the adjusted scores, ask whether you could build the capability in-house, and make your own adopt, pilot, or pass call based on the tradeoffs you can accept.
