# Demo Output — Score One Tool Against the Framework

*Actual Claude output — the framework applied to one AI ad-creative adaptation tool, then a build-vs-buy check. One run; outputs vary.*

**Tool scored:** **Framecast**, an AI ad-creative adaptation tool — hand it a master creative and it produces every placement size, recropping, reflowing type and repositioning the logo per frame. **The real task:** our monthly paid-social refresh, 40 masters × 14 placement specs = 560 exports, currently a contract designer's week. Scale 1 (poor) – 5 (excellent).

| Dimension | Score | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|
| Capability fit | 5 | Ran the full 560 twice. Every export at spec, safe zones respected, logo never clipped, no failures, and the second run matched the first. Does the job and does it dependably. |
| Brand-voice control / brand-safety | 3 | The brand kit locks fonts, colours and logo clear-space, so nothing leaves looking off-brand. Two gaps: on roughly 1 in 10 verticals the crop pushed the product out of frame, and it reflows legal disclaimers to whatever size fits with nothing checking they're still legible. Every file hit its spec — whether the crop still shows the product is a brand judgment, so it scores here, not under capability. |
| Fit with your stack | 4 | Pulls masters from our asset library and pushes finished sizes into two of the three ad platforms we run. The third is still a manual export. |
| Cost vs. value | 5 | Flat team rate, not per-seat, with placement volume included. Measured against the baseline — the contractor week we already pay for — it costs roughly a fifth as much. |
| Data & privacy | 2 | Uploads live on their servers, and the standard terms let them use uploaded creative to improve their models. Opt-out exists only on the enterprise tier. Assets are exportable at any time. |
| Learning curve / adoption | 5 | Half a day of brand-kit setup, then it's one step in a workflow the team already runs. Nothing to retrain. |
| **Total** | **24** | |

**Stress-testing a score (with Claude):** *"I gave data & privacy a 2 because they can train on our uploaded creative — is that enough to pass on the tool?"* → The 2 is right, but it isn't load-bearing: these are ads about to be published to the entire internet, so training exposure costs us little. It becomes decisive the day unreleased product shots go through it.

**Build-vs-buy check:** the same masters and the same 14 specs, through the general models and image tooling already on our bill. Close on a single frame; unusable at 560 — type re-rendered instead of preserving the brand font, logo position drifting, pixel dimensions unreliable. Checking and fixing by hand costs more than the contractor. **The baseline loses here — and it had to be run to know that.**

**Decision size:** not quite a two-way door. The brand kit and all 14 placement specs get built inside the tool and the monthly workflow reroutes around it, so cancelling means rebuilding the setup and returning to contractor weeks. That's why the privacy score got argued with rather than waved through. The vendor is five years old and profitable, so durability isn't the blocker.

**Verdict:** **Adopt, with two guardrails.** No unreleased product creative through it until the no-training tier is priced, and disclaimer legibility plus the vertical hero crops stay a human check before publish. **Tradeoff accepted:** a 2 on data & privacy, taken knowingly.

*The value isn't the total — it's this documented reasoning you can hand a skeptical manager.*
