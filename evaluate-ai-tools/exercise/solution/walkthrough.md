# Solution — Evaluate the Field, Make a Recommendation

*Worked solution, one strong example. The scores are judgment calls from the provided profiles; a defensible submission may weight differently and reach a different call, as long as the reasoning holds. Scale: 1 (poor) to 5 (excellent).*

## 1. Score the three tools against the rubric

| Dimension | Penmark | CopyFlow | BrandForge |
|---|---|---|---|
| Capability fit | 5 | 2 | 4 |
| Brand-voice control / brand-safety | 4 | 2 | 5 |
| Fit with your stack | 1 | 4 | 3 |
| Cost vs. value | 3 | 4 | 3 |
| Data & privacy | 5 | 5 | 2 |
| Learning curve / adoption | 3 | 5 | 2 |

**Read the shape, not just the totals.** Penmark and CopyFlow are near-opposites: best copy and worst integration versus worst copy and best integration. BrandForge is the only one pairing strong brand-voice control with an actual compliance check, but it has the weakest privacy story unless you pay up for the no-training tier.

## 2. Review the scores

The profiles are diligence findings, not verdicts. A few are worth pushing on:
- **CopyFlow cost (4, not 5):** cheap per seat, but generic output creates an editing burden that never shows on the invoice. Cheap in cash, less cheap in your team's time.
- **BrandForge cost (3):** $199/mo flat looks great until you need the no-training tier, which flips to per-seat and lands above Penmark. Score the version you would actually buy.
- **Penmark stack fit (1):** no connectors, everything is copy-paste. Real friction for a stretched team.

## 3. Weight for this team

This team is lean and stretched thin, so the binding constraints are **cost vs. value** and **learning curve / adoption**. Weight those most heavily. (A regulated brand would weight brand-safety and data privacy heavily instead, and could reach a different call, that is the point of weighting.)

## 4. Build-vs-buy

Before recommending a purchase, weigh the option you already have: a general AI, with a good brief, doing the job. On everyday copy it often matches the mid-tier tools at no marginal cost. Where it loses is the plumbing, no integration and no brand-safety guardrails or audit trail. "Buy nothing new" is a legitimate result and should stay on the table.

## 5. The recommendation

**Recommend CopyFlow**, on the two dimensions this team weighted heaviest.
- **Cost vs. value:** roughly $54/month for three seats, against ~$360 for Penmark and ~$447-plus for BrandForge's no-training tier. An order of magnitude cheaper.
- **Learning curve / adoption:** nothing to configure, no voice-sample set to maintain, no rollout to own. The team can start today. Penmark needs an ongoing sample set; BrandForge needs a two-week rollout with an owner, a real ask for a stretched team.

**Main concern:** CopyFlow is weakest on capability fit and brand-voice control. Output skews generic and tone drifts across a batch, so it needs a human edit pass before it ships.

**Why it is manageable:** it ships a configurable banned-words and blocked-topics filter (the only vocabulary-level guardrail in the field), a short human review is already part of how this team ships copy, and we would run a 30-day pilot that measures edit time per piece, and revisit if clean-up erodes the cost advantage.

*(A submission that weights capability and brand-safety instead, and recommends BrandForge or "buy nothing," is equally strong if the reasoning holds.)*

## Common mistakes

- Letting the highest raw total win automatically, instead of weighting what matters for this team.
- Never weighing the build-vs-buy option, so "buy nothing" is never considered.
- Treating brand-voice and brand-safety as one thing. Penmark locks voice tightly and checks claims not at all.
- Scoring cost off a sticker instead of a shape (per-seat versus flat at your real team size and volume).
- A recommendation with no named concern. That is a summary, not a decision.
