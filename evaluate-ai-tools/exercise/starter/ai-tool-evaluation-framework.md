# AI Tool Evaluation Framework (Scorecard)

*Provided framework for the "Evaluate and Select AI Tools" exercise. Score each candidate on the dimensions below, then use the scores plus your judgment to reach a decision. The highest total does not automatically win. The point is to make the tradeoffs visible and end with a clear call.*

---

## Step 1 — Start with build-vs-buy

Before you score any product, ask whether you can already do the job with the general AI you have. A good brief plus a tool you already pay for often beats a new subscription. Run your real task through a couple of models you already have and treat that as your **baseline**. Any specialized tool has to clearly beat the baseline to earn its cost and its spot in your stack. "We don't need to buy anything" is a legitimate result.

---

## Step 2 — Score the candidates

Score each dimension 1 to 5 (1 = poor fit, 5 = excellent fit). Note the reasoning, not just the number. Score the baseline on the same dimensions so the comparison is honest.

| Dimension | What you're judging |
|---|---|
| **Capability fit** | Does it do the specific job well, and do it dependably? Judge it on your real task, not the demo. |
| **Fit with your stack** | Does it connect to the tools you already run, or is it manual copy-paste? Does it duplicate something you already have? Fit beats horsepower. |
| **Cost vs. value** | Is the price justified by what it does that you couldn't otherwise do? Measure it against your baseline, not against zero. |
| **Data & privacy** | Where does your data go? Is it used to train their models? Can you get your data back out? |
| **Learning curve / adoption** | How hard is it for you and your team to actually use, roll out, and maintain? A powerful tool nobody adopts is a weak choice. |
| **Brand-voice control / brand-safety** | Can you keep the output on-brand and compliant? Does the tool let you lock tone, restrict vocabulary, or apply brand-safety rules — or does output drift off-brand across a batch? This is the marketing-specific dimension generic software checklists miss. |

Not every dimension carries equal weight for every tool. Some tools produce a clean, measurable output you can judge directly. Others do something fuzzier, where capability is more of a judgment call than a score. Weight the dimensions that actually matter for the job in front of you, and say which ones you weighted and why.

---

## Step 3 — Size the decision (is it a two-way door?)

Match your rigor to the stakes, and factor in how durable the tool is likely to be.

- A cheap, cancel-anytime tool is a **two-way door**. You can walk back through it easily, so it only needs a quick look, even from a young or unproven vendor.
- A tool you would build your workflow around, or that involves migration and lock-in, is a **one-way door**. It demands real scrutiny on fit, cost, data, and especially **stability**. How likely is this vendor to still be here and supported in two years? Specific tools come and go, so the more permanent the commitment, the more the vendor's durability matters.

---

## Step 4 — End with a clear call

Every evaluation finishes with a decision, not a vibe. For each candidate (and the baseline), land on one:

- **Adopt** — onboard it now.
- **Pilot** — test it in a small, low-stakes trial first.
- **Pass** — walk away (including "just use the baseline").

Name the call and the one tradeoff you accepted to get there.

---

## What to produce

A short recommendation memo: the candidates, the scored comparison (including the DIY baseline), which dimensions you weighted and why, your chosen call for each, and the tradeoff behind it.
