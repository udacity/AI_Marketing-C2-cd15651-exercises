# Cobbleway — Brand and Copy Task

*Provided asset for this exercise. This is the brand whose copy the tool has to write, and the one piece you will use to test every candidate. Score **capability fit** and **brand-voice control / brand-safety** on this task, not on a vendor demo.*

---

## The brand

Cobbleway makes everyday rain gear for people who cycle or walk to work: a packable commuter shell, waterproof overtrousers, a shoe cover, a pannier cover. Not technical mountain kit. Clothes you can wear into a meeting.

**Positioning:** dressed for the weather, not dressed for an expedition.

## Who we talk to

City commuters, roughly 28 to 50, who ride or walk to work most days and would rather not own a second wardrobe. They already know it rains. They are practical buyers who read the material spec, care about fit and packability, and are tired of outdoor brands selling them a summit they will never climb.

## Voice

- **Plain and specific.** Name the material, the fit, the actual use. Don't let an adjective do a spec's job.
- **Dry and understated.** Weather is a fact you dress for, not an enemy you defeat. Light humour is welcome; enthusiasm is not.
- **Second person, low pressure.** You're telling a commuter something useful, not selling to an athlete.
- **Honest about limits.** Say what the product doesn't do — waterproof, not warm. The candour is the brand.

**Avoid:** "conquer," "brave the elements," "unstoppable," "gear up," "adventure," "performance"; exclamation marks; emoji; weather-fear; discounts; re-explaining what rain is.

## The test piece

One email, to be run through every candidate and the baseline:

- **Segment:** bought the Commuter Shell 8 to 14 months ago, nothing since.
- **Goal:** one click through to the Overtrousers page. Not a reply, not a browse.
- **Context:** the wet season is starting. The overtrousers pack into a jersey pocket, go on over work trousers without taking your shoes off, and cost less than the shell. Recipients already own and like the shell.
- **Format:** subject under 45 characters, body under 70 words, one bracketed CTA.

Turn this into a brief yourself — the raw material is above, the brief is your work. Then run the *same* brief through each candidate and through a couple of general models, and judge the drafts against the voice and avoid lists. One email is enough to see capability; watch also for what a single email *can't* tell you about voice control across a batch.
