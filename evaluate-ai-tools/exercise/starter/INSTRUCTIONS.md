# Compare the Field, Write the Memo

You run marketing for Cobbleway, a small rain-gear brand, and you need to recommend how your three-person team should cover its copywriting work. Evaluate three specialized AI copywriting tools and one DIY baseline against a shared framework, compare them head to head, and produce a recommendation memo a manager could act on.

Work in Claude. Use:

- [`ai-tool-evaluation-framework.md`](ai-tool-evaluation-framework.md) — the scorecard.
- [`tool-profiles.md`](tool-profiles.md) — your diligence findings on the candidates.
- [`brand-and-copy-task.md`](brand-and-copy-task.md) — the brand, its voice, and the one copy task you test everything on.

No single option wins on every dimension, and the specialized tools have to justify their cost and risk against the baseline you may already have.

## What to produce

A recommendation memo containing:

- Each of the three specialized tools scored against the framework dimensions, using the profiles as your findings (optionally enriched with light web research).
- The DIY-LLM baseline scored on the same dimensions, and tested hands-on by running the Cobbleway copy task through a couple of models with a brief you write from the brand asset.
- A four-way head-to-head comparison and a chosen winner.
- The key tradeoff you accepted — which dimension your winner lost on, and why that was acceptable.
- The call for your winner: **adopt now** or **pilot first**.

## Requirements

- Score the baseline on the same dimensions as the tools. The comparison is only honest if the free-ish option is measured, not assumed.
- Test the baseline live across a couple of models, on the Cobbleway test piece — comparing them hands-on is a check no profile can telegraph. Judge the drafts against the brand's voice and avoid lists, not against your taste.
- Say what your cost scores assume. Three of the dimensions describe properties, not verdicts; a per-seat rate and a flat rate rank differently depending on the team size and volume you price for.
- "Buy nothing new" is a legitimate recommendation. If a specialized tool wins, state what it does that the general LLM can't; if the baseline wins, say so plainly.
- Say which dimensions you weighted most heavily for this job, and why. Not every dimension matters equally.

## Done when

Your memo names one option, the tradeoff behind it, and whether it's adopt-or-pilot — and a skeptical manager could act on it without re-doing your analysis.
