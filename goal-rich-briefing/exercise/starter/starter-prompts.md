# Goal-Rich Brief Exercise — Starter Prompts

*Provided input for the "Rewrite a Generic Prompt as a Goal-Rich Marketing Brief" exercise.*

**The product:** Flowline is a freemium productivity app where people manage their projects and tasks. The free tier is generous. Premium adds cross-device sync, unlimited projects, and workflow shortcuts that make the app noticeably faster to use. You run lifecycle and conversion messaging.

Below are three situations. Each comes with a starter prompt that is deliberately weak, and each is weak in a *different* way. Your job is to diagnose how each one fails and rewrite it into a goal-rich brief.

---

## Situation 1 — Checkout abandonment recovery email

A user clicked upgrade, reached the payment screen for the annual Premium plan, and left without completing the purchase.

**Starter prompt (as provided):**
> "Write a checkout-recovery email. Exactly four sentences. Open with a question. Mention our 20-percent-faster workflow. Add a countdown timer. End with a P.S."

---

## Situation 2 — New-user activation in-app message

A user signed up for the free tier three days ago but has never completed the first core action, creating their first project, so they have not yet experienced what the product is for.

**Starter prompt (as provided):**
> "Write an in-app message for new users."

---

## Situation 3 — Power-user referral

A segment of paying users who log in weekly and use Flowline heavily.

**Starter prompt (as provided):**
> "Make our best users feel appreciated."
