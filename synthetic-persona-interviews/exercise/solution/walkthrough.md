# Solution — Design Three, Interview Three, Separate Signal from Noise

*Worked solution — one strong example. The personas, quotes, and interview excerpts below are illustrative of what good work looks like; a student's will differ. What matters: every trait traced to research, both confabulation markers used, and a synthesis that finds cross-persona patterns.*

## Three seeded personas (each trait tied to research)

**1. Maya — design-focused apartment dweller.** Small apartment, everything on display; wants a bed that reads as decor. *Traceable to:* "I shove it in the closet when people come over"; "I'd pay a premium for one that actually looked like furniture."

**2. Rick — practical multi-dog household.** Three dogs incl. a chewer; durability and washability over looks. *Traceable to:* "the only bed that's survived a year"; "if it can't go in the machine, it's useless."

**3. Priya — new puppy owner.** 11-week puppy, accidents and chewing; wants a safe, cheap starter, will invest later. *Traceable to:* "didn't want to spend a fortune yet"; "all about easy cleanup."

Those three lines are the summary, not the deliverable. A full answer fills every field of the seed template for each persona and completes its evidence-links table, so each trait sits next to the quote it came from — [`example-output.md`](example-output.md) shows one persona worked all the way through that way.

**Flagged as assumptions:** the names, the ages, and the pet details the research never states — Maya's dog Juniper, and the "11-week" precision on Priya's puppy — are **not** traceable. The research provides owner types and quotes, with no names, ages or breeds. Note what is *not* on that list: Rick's "three dogs incl. a chewer" is quoted research ("Three dogs, one of them a chewer"), and Priya's accidents-and-chewing puppy is quoted research too, so neither gets flagged. Nor does the trust channel each persona names when asked who they would believe — the research carries an influence quote for all three owner types, so those answers are grounded rather than invented. Check each detail against the snippets rather than flagging a whole category — over-flagging a grounded trait is the same error as under-flagging an invented one. Do this in your own answer: invent what you need to make a persona interviewable, then label it, and keep your findings off it. The brief asks you to flag what you can't tie to evidence, and this is what that looks like.

## Interview highlights (furniture-grade modern concept)

All ten core questions were run with each persona; what follows is the concept-reaction slice (Q4, Q5, Q6, Q8). The warm-up questions matter more than they look — Q1 and Q2 are what later let you tell a grounded answer from an invented one, because you can check a persona's objection against the shopping behaviour they described before they saw the concept. See [`example-output.md`](example-output.md) for the fuller per-question excerpts.

- **Maya —** *resonates:* "finally, something I wouldn't hide." *Falls flat:* nothing much — she's the target. *Missing:* proof it's actually well made, not just marked-up design.
- **Rick —** *resonates:* little. *Falls flat:* "furniture-grade" reads as delicate/precious. *Missing:* durability + washability answers; won't consider it until those are addressed.
- **Priya —** *resonates:* the look, aspirationally. *Falls flat:* price for a puppy phase. *Missing:* reassurance it survives a puppy, or a reason to buy now vs. later.

## Signal vs. confabulation (both markers)

| Persona | Trustworthy signal (grounded) | Likely confabulation | Marker |
|---|---|---|---|
| Maya | Hides current bed; wants decor-like → real | *"I'd pay up to $200, no problem"* | specificity beyond the seed — the research says "pay a premium," never a figure |
| Rick | Durability/washability gate everything → real | *"I'd definitely buy two by next month"* | behavioral prediction never experienced |
| Priya | Cost-sensitive during puppy phase → real | *"I'd tell all my new-mom friends"* | behavioral prediction never experienced |

## Synthesis (the hard part)

**Where they agree:** the *look* lands as appealing with two of the three (Maya, Priya) — the concept's aesthetic promise is real signal. Rick is the exception: "furniture-grade" reads as delicate to him, not desirable.

**Where they split:** willingness to buy divides on **proof and price**. Maya is ready if quality is credible; Rick is a hard no until durability/washability are answered; Priya likes it but won't pay during the puppy phase.

**Recurring objection (stronger signal):** two of the three — Maya and Rick — doubt a "designer" bed is actually *well made*. Priya's worry is adjacent rather than the same one: she doesn't question the make, she questions whether anything survives a puppy. It still lands in the same place — proof of durability, not styling, is the gate for all three — but for two different reasons. Say two-of-three when it is two-of-three; rounding it up to "all three" is how a synthesis quietly overstates its own evidence.

**What I'd carry into real testing:** lead with the look for design-minded urban owners (Maya is the beachhead), but pair every aesthetic claim with hard durability/washability proof — that's what converts the skeptics and de-risks the "pretty but flimsy" fear. Don't chase Rick's segment with this line; don't expect Priya to buy until post-puppy.

**Which new market fits:** design-conscious urban owners (Maya) — the furniture-grade concept lands there and struggles elsewhere.

**Confidence:** medium. Three synthetic personas are directional; the look-appeal and the make-quality objection each recurred in two of the three interviews — consistent enough to treat as signal, but not universal. Segment sizes need live research.

## Common mistakes

- Inventing traits with no research backing (should be flagged as assumptions).
- Running all three in one conversation (anchoring) instead of fresh chats.
- A synthesis that lists each persona instead of finding the shared pattern and the real divergence.
- Skipping the warm-up and opening with the concept. Q1 and Q2 are the baseline you audit the rest against; without them a persona's objection has nothing to be checked against, and confabulation gets much harder to spot.
