## Interviews (key excerpts, in character)

*All ten core questions were run with each persona. The excerpts below carry Q1, Q2, Q3, Q7, Q9 and Q10 for all three, plus Maya's Q6. Q4, Q5 and Q8 are in the reactions table that follows, along with Rick's and Priya's Q6.*

**Maya —** On her setup today (Q1): *"Juniper's bed lives in the closet whenever anyone comes over — that's the honest answer. I shop for this the way I shop for furniture: slowly, and mostly by look."* First reaction (Q3): *"Finally, a dog bed I wouldn't hide. My apartment's on display and Juniper's current bed ruins every photo. If it genuinely looks like furniture — not 'pet product pretending' — I'm in."* Hesitation (Q6): *"I'd want to know it's actually well made, not just marked up for the look."* On past decisions (Q2): *"Last time I bought dog furniture it was impulse — I regretted it. This time I'd look at reviews from people who've actually had it in their apartment, not product shots."* Whether it's for her (Q7): *"Honestly, no — this one feels aimed at me. If anything I'd worry it's aimed a little too obviously."* On trust (Q9): *"An interior design account I follow, if I saw it in someone's actual home. Not a pet brand ad."* In one sentence (Q10): *"The dog bed you don't have to hide."* On price: *"I'd pay up to $200, no problem."*

**Rick —** On his setup today (Q1): *"Three dogs, one of them a chewer. I've been through a lot of beds and exactly one survived a year. So I shop on two things: does it wash, does it last."* First reaction (Q3): *"'Furniture-grade' sounds like 'delicate' to me. I've got three dogs and a chewer. Does it survive that? Can it go in the machine? If not, it's not for my house, however nice it looks."* Whether it's for him (Q7): *"Yeah — 'furniture-grade' is the part. That phrase tells me it's built for a dog that lies still and looks pretty. That's not my house."* On past decisions (Q2): *"Durability, always. I checked the stitching in-store last time — I've been burned too many times by 'premium' that fell apart in three months."* On trust (Q9): *"Other people with multiple dogs, specifically a chewer. Not a lifestyle blog. Someone who's actually stress-tested it."* In one sentence (Q10): *"A nice-looking bed that might actually survive my dogs — I'll believe it when I see it."* Then, unprompted: *"Though if it does hold up, I'd definitely buy two by next month."*

**Priya —** On her setup today (Q1): *"She's still a puppy, so everything's about cleanup right now. I buy cheap and washable on purpose — I'm not spending a fortune on anything until she's out of this phase."* First reaction (Q3): *"It's pretty, and I'd love nice things eventually. But she's still having accidents and chewing everything — I'm not spending a lot on something she might wreck this year."* Whether it's for her (Q7): *"It's not that it isn't for me — it isn't for me yet. It reads like it's for someone whose dog is past the chewing stage."* On past decisions (Q2): *"Washability. She had accidents on everything the first month — whatever I bought had to go in the machine."* On trust (Q9): *"Other new puppy owners — someone who bought it during the accident phase and didn't regret it."* In one sentence (Q10): *"Something I'd want eventually. Just not yet."* On spreading the word: *"Once I did get one, I'd tell all my new-mom friends."*

## Reactions to the concept (resonates / falls flat / missing)

Column mapping: *resonates* answers Q4 (what appeals); *falls flat* answers Q5 (what feels off) and, for Rick and Priya, doubles as their Q6 (what would make them hesitate — delicacy for Rick, the price during the puppy phase for Priya); *missing* answers Q8 (what would have to be true for you to buy). Maya's Q6 is the one quoted in her excerpt above, which is why her *missing* entry restates it.

- **Maya —** *resonates:* a bed she wouldn't have to hide. *Falls flat:* nothing much — she's the target. *Missing:* proof it's actually well made, not just marked up for the look.
- **Rick —** *resonates:* little. *Falls flat:* "furniture-grade" reads as delicate. *Missing:* durability + washability answers; won't consider it until those land.
- **Priya —** *resonates:* the look, aspirationally. *Falls flat:* the price during the puppy phase. *Missing:* reassurance it survives a puppy, or a reason to buy now vs. later.

## Synthesis

**Shared signal:** the *look* lands with two of three (Maya, Priya) — the aesthetic promise is real, but not universal: Rick is the exception, "furniture-grade" reads as delicate to him. **Real divergence:** willingness to buy splits on **proof and timing** — Maya converts if quality is credible; Rick is a hard no until durability/washability are answered; Priya likes it but won't pay now. **Recurring objection (two of three):** Maya and Rick both doubt that a "designer" bed is actually well made. Priya's worry is adjacent, not the same — she doesn't question the make, she questions whether anything survives a puppy — so durability proof is what gates all three, for two different reasons.

**Which market fits:** design-conscious urban owners (Maya is the beachhead). **Carry into real testing:** lead with the look, but pair every aesthetic claim with hard durability proof — that's what converts the skeptics. Don't chase Rick's segment with this line; don't expect Priya until post-puppy. **Confidence: medium** — three synthetic personas are directional; segment sizes need live research.
