# Synthesis Example (what "good" looks like)

*Provided model for the hardest step of the exercise: synthesizing across interviews. This is an example from a different concept, so it shows the shape and depth of a strong synthesis without handing you the answer for yours. Your synthesis should do the same job: find the patterns, name the divergences, and land a clear read, all tied to what personas actually said.*

---

## Example concept tested: a subscription "treat box" for dogs

*(Illustrative only. Three personas interviewed: a budget-minded multi-dog owner, a premium-focused single-dog owner, and a new puppy owner.)*

### Where they agreed

All three personas liked the convenience of not having to remember to restock treats. That was the one consistent pull across very different owners. Convenience, not novelty, was the real draw.

### Where they split

The concept divided sharply on price sensitivity. The premium single-dog owner was happy to pay for curation and quality and saw the subscription as a treat for herself as much as the dog. The budget multi-dog owner did the math immediately and balked: with three dogs, a monthly box felt like a luxury she could not justify against buying in bulk. The new puppy owner sat in the middle, interested but cautious, because she was not yet sure what her puppy could even eat.

### The objection that showed up more than once

Two of the three raised control: they wanted to choose or at least veto what came in the box, worried about allergies or ingredients they avoid. A fully curated, hands-off box made them nervous.

### What I would carry into real testing

The convenience angle is the strongest and most universal hook, so lead with it. But the concept as framed (fully curated, fixed monthly) alienates the budget and control-sensitive segments. Worth testing a version with customization and a flexible cadence, and worth deciding early whether this is a premium play for the single-dog owner or a mass play, because it cannot easily be both.

### Confidence

Medium. Three personas is directional, not definitive. The price and control objections were consistent enough to trust as real signal. The size of each segment is a question only live research can answer.

---

**Notes on what makes this a good synthesis:**
- It separates a shared pattern (convenience) from a real divergence (price sensitivity), rather than just listing what each persona said.
- It flags an objection that recurred across personas, which is stronger signal than a one-off.
- It ends with a decision and a next step, not just observations.
- It states confidence and limits honestly.
