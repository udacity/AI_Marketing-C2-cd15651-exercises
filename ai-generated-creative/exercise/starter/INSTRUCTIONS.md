# Take the Brand Into a New Market

Vessl is breaking into a new market: boxing and MMA gyms. The creative can't look like the usual bright, calm, daylight brand; it has to feel intense enough to belong in a serious athlete's gym, while staying recognizably Vessl. And it's not a paid social post this time, it's a printed direct-mail flyer sent to gyms across the country. Direct the concept, generate the flyer, iterate at least once, and deliver a memo on the direction you chose.

Work in **Claude + an AI image tool of your choice** (any AI image generator you can access). Claude directs; the image tool executes; you carry the context between them. Use:

- [`brand-guide.md`](brand-guide.md), the core brand: voice, color palette (with hex), typography, look.
- [`gym-campaign-brief.md`](gym-campaign-brief.md), the "In Your Corner" campaign: objective, audience, message, format.
- [`campaign-aesthetic-addendum.md`](campaign-aesthetic-addendum.md), how the brand flexes for this campaign (darker and grittier) and what stays sacred.

**Scope note:** flyers get copy-heavy, and this exercise is about the **visual creative direction**, not writing flyer copy. Keep the tagline "Hydration, handled." fixed and use placeholder text (lorem ipsum) for any headline, body, and CTA, so you can judge composition and hierarchy without getting bogged down in copywriting.

## What to produce

- The Claude direction you used, and the self-contained image prompt(s) you wrote.
- A flyer concept generated in your image tool, then **iterated at least once** across tools (generate, critique against the brand guide and addendum, refine the prompt, regenerate).
- A short **decision memo**: the visual direction you're leading with, why it fits the brief and the aesthetic addendum while staying on brand, what else you considered and set aside, and the one improvement your iteration made.

## Requirements

- Assemble the full input stack (brand guide + campaign brief + aesthetic addendum) before you direct. The addendum is the key input here: it tells Claude how the brand flexes for this market.
- Land the concept first, then write a **self-contained** image prompt that bakes in the addendum's palette (deep Marine / near-black grounds, Vessl Aqua as a charged accent, Coral reserved for the CTA), the dramatic gym lighting, and the gritty candid athlete imagery, since the image tool won't remember anything Claude knows.
- Hold the brand while flexing the look: however dark and intense the flyer gets, Vessl Aqua must be present and identifying, the tagline stays, no fear or guilt framing, and the bottle reads as part of the training kit, not a staged product shot.
- Run at least one full critique loop across tools, and critique against the brand guide and the addendum, not just taste.
- Keep copy as placeholder; you're aligning on visual direction, not final wording.

## Done when

You have a flyer visual direction that reads as intense and gym-native yet unmistakably Vessl, evidence you iterated to improve it, and a memo that states the direction, the reasoning, and what you set aside, that a marketing lead could act on.
