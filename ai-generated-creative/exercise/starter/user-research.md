## Who they are
Active, health-aware, 25 to 45. Most already track steps or sleep with a watch or ring. They see themselves as "trying to feel good," not as gadget people or biohackers.

## What we heard

- **Hydration is the habit that falls through the cracks.** They've "solved" movement and sleep with wearables; water is the one thing they still forget, and they feel mildly guilty about it.
- **They're motivated by how they feel, not by data.** Energy, focus, and an even mood are what they want. Charts, streaks, and numbers for their own sake leave them cold.
- **"One less thing to manage" is the strongest phrase we tested.** This audience feels low-grade optimization fatigue. Anything that removes effort beats anything that adds a task.
- **Fear and guilt backfire.** "You're dehydrated" / "you're falling behind" framing made people defensive and read as nagging. Positive, effortless framing outperformed it every time.
- **They trust real over polished.** Candid, everyday moments (a real desk, a real gym bag, natural light) read as authentic; glossy studio product shots read as "an ad" and got skipped.

## What this means for creative
Lead with the feeling of an easier day, not the technology. Show the bottle in a real moment, not on a pedestal. Keep it warm and light. Never scold.
